Organisation, users and operations

The case study is a simulated online retailer selling clothing, footwear and accessories. Customers browse variants, manage carts, place orders and track purchases. Administrators maintain products, stock and fulfilment; management uses sales and inventory reports. Major data includes customers, addresses, products, categories, brands, sizes, colours, variants, inventory, carts, orders, items, payment attempts and deliveries.

One scenario connects all five activities. PostgreSQL owns transactional prices, stock and purchase records. MongoDB provides flexible descriptive product enrichment. Data is synthetic; payment outcomes are simulated. No live storefront, payment gateway or cloud deployment is claimed.

1 · Database design and modelling

The relational design contains 16 tables with primary/foreign keys, uniqueness, required values and validation rules. A table-style ERD shows attributes, keys, cardinalities and optionality. Normalisation explains 1NF–3NF and deliberate purchase-time snapshots: later catalogue or address changes must not rewrite an earlier purchase.

PostgreSQL 18.1 implements the schema, integrity triggers, controlled write routines and a restricted demonstration writer role. Checks cover invalid references, duplicate variants, negative stock, invalid prices, lifecycle transitions and checkout consistency. The sample has 12 customers, eight products, 18 variants and 12 orders; recognised sales reconcile to NGN 268,000.

Evidence: ERD image · schema, normalisation and outputs. Fresh checks: 35 integrity/workflow, 10 sample and four scale assertions.

2 · Query processing and optimisation

Twelve reports cover catalogue filtering, stock, customer history, fulfilment, revenue and sales trends. Revenue uses historical purchase amounts and eligible order states; failed payment retries do not duplicate revenue. Thirty result checks cover totals, empty groups, date endpoints and timezone behaviour.

Five reports were measured on a synthetic 10,012-order dataset before indexing, after three reporting indexes, and after a targeted history rewrite. Each phase used a warm-up and five measured plans. Complete ordered-result fingerprints matched. The rewrite aggregates payment attempts for selected customer orders instead of scanning every attempt.

3 · Transactions and concurrency

A controlled checkout transaction combines order creation, item snapshots, simulated successful payment and inventory deduction. A NGN 49,000 purchase passed eight persistence checks after COMMIT. An injected pre-commit failure followed by ROLLBACK passed six checks for no purchase rows and restored stock. Two persistent sessions passed seven READ COMMITTED visibility checks.

An isolated unsafe last-unit model let two buyers read stock one and commit two purchases, demonstrating why nonnegative stock alone does not prevent overselling. The protected checkout made the second buyer wait on the first, then rejected the depleted-stock purchase. Eleven checks confirmed one successful buyer, stock conservation and unchanged state on replay of known-order finalisation. That replay does not make new-order requests or external charges idempotent.

4 · NoSQL and advanced data models

MongoDB Community 8.0.34 stores three product-enrichment profiles with embedded category-specific materials, care and descriptive features. String IDs preserve SQL product identity. Strict validation checks document shape; importer/reconciliation checks enforce source matching and rules such as material percentages. PostgreSQL retains authority over stock, price, orders and payments.

The fresh import passed 22 checks plus four checks through a new connection. Five finds and a tag aggregation passed 16 checks. A compound multikey index was tested: a hinted diagnostic examined one document and preserved results, but the automatic planner retained the identity scan on this tiny collection. No speedup is claimed. The SQL-to-MongoDB workflow reconciles one exported snapshot; live synchronisation is not implemented.

Evidence: model, implementation and queries · SQL/NoSQL suitability and limits.

5 · Distributed and cloud databases

The permitted discussion path proposes one region with three independent zones. PostgreSQL has one writer, a required synchronous standby and an asynchronous reporting standby. Recent-order reads use the current writer; lagging reports cannot authorise a purchase. Safe failover requires exclusive authority, fencing of the former writer and validated recovery. Loss of the required standby can stall checkout commits.

A three-member MongoDB replica set is proposed for enrichment. Database replication would not refresh copied SQL facts. Sharding is deferred: there is no production capacity evidence requiring it, and a basket spanning inventory shards would need coordinated transactions or an explicit reservation/recovery design. Eight scenarios cover failures, stale reads, ambiguous commits and recovery. All topology and failure behaviour remain proposed and untested.

Evidence: architecture diagram and scenarios · decisions and references.

Individual technical reflection

I directed changes to the ERD presentation by supplying a table-style reference, reviewing the revised appearance and requesting explicit explanations of relationship ratios. Presenting keys and attributes alongside one-to-one and one-to-many relationships makes the model easier to inspect: the drawing needs to communicate business rules as well as table names. I also requested an image without the rendering script, and kept development staged through checklist updates and progressive commits.

The technical evidence illustrates why a plausible result needs a stronger check. Nonnegative stock did not prevent two purchases in the unsafe race; the protected test also checked purchase counts and stock conservation. Similarly, query timings were interpreted alongside unchanged results and execution plans. These are useful principles for future database work: specify the business invariant, test failure cases, and retain evidence for each claimed improvement. My recorded review covers the ERD presentation. The runtime tests were executed through Codex; independently reproducing and explaining them remains an area for further personal verification.
